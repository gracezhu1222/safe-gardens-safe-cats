Our original research · 2026

From curiosity to evidence.

We combined botanical observations, published plant-safety resources, a community survey, and a veterinarian interview to identify educational priorities.

Field study

15 plants examined.

Our student team visited San Diego Botanic Garden to observe plants, read plant labels, and photograph species for the project. Garden education staff helped us examine the identification process.

Garden observation is not evidence of how common a plant is in every San Diego household.

Community survey

What 114 responses told us.

114 People surveyed
38 Respondents with cats
30 Cat owners reporting cats could access plants at least sometimes

Among the 38 cat owners, 22 reported their cats had touched or played with plants. Four of 37 people answering a question about signs after plant contact reported signs; none were verified as plant-caused illnesses by a veterinarian.

Limitation: Participants were recruited through social/community networks, a class, and in-person contacts. The sample was not randomly selected, and the counts do not represent San Diego-wide poisoning rates or plant prevalence.

Veterinary perspective

Learning from a local veterinarian.

On April 8, 2026, the team interviewed Dr. Ellis at Academy Animal Hospital about possible plant exposures and prevention. The interview offered perspective, not a population-wide case count or medical study.

Our website is educational. If exposure is suspected, contact a veterinarian or animal poison-control professional rather than relying on this project to diagnose symptoms.

Preliminary model

What is SD-CPRI?

The San Diego Cat Plant Risk Index was an early concept intended to combine toxicity, presence, exposure, and awareness to inform which topics deserve more public education. We did not finalize the weighting rules or clinically validate the model.

Therefore, this site does not show a numerical risk ranking or claim to predict poisoning for individual cats.
