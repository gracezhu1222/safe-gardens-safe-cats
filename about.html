About the project

Research driven by curiosity.
Shared for cat safety.

From studying plants in our own homes to photographing specimens and talking with a veterinarian, our students set out to better understand the plants around cats in San Diego.

Our story

One question started it all.

Our original question was simple: could a plant near home hurt a curious cat? We researched plant names and veterinary guidance, collected observations at San Diego Botanic Garden, talked with local residents, and interviewed a veterinarian.

The team recorded observations of 15 plants and surveyed 114 people about plant presence, cat access, and awareness. The study helps identify educational questions; it does not estimate rates of poisoning across San Diego.

Original research

What we actually studied

114People answered the survey, recruited through community and school networks.
38Respondents said they owned cats.
30Of those cat owners said their cats could reach plants at least sometimes.

22 cat owners reported that their cats had touched or played with plants. Four survey reports described signs after plant contact, but no veterinarian confirmed a plant caused those signs. These are descriptive survey responses, not clinical outcomes.

Our early SD-CPRI concept

SD-CPRI stands for San Diego Cat Plant Risk Index. The team explored four educational dimensions: toxicity, presence, exposure, and awareness. The precise scoring weights were not finalized, and the index has not been validated as a tool to predict poisoning or determine an individual cat's risk. We therefore show plant categories and source-specific survey counts, not numeric clinical-risk predictions.

How we worked

Project milestones

Research and plant identification

Reviewed plant names and cat-toxicity references and recorded field observations at San Diego Botanic Garden, with input from its education staff.

Community survey

Gathered 114 responses through WeChat, a school class, a supermarket, a church, and a park. The sample was voluntary and not representative of all San Diego cat owners.

Veterinarian interview · April 8, 2026

Met Dr. Ellis at Academy Animal Hospital. The interview explored how cats are exposed to plants and why professional advice matters after possible exposure.

Manuscript and public education

Prepared Safe Gardens, Safe Cats, a student book manuscript with research, plant profiles, and prevention tips. The team also planned a December 2026 library presentation.
